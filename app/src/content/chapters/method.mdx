## The PFD–GSTE Method

The model family combines a ResNet50V2 convolutional branch with a rotation-aware Vision Transformer branch. Residual CNNs provide strong local feature extraction [@he2016identity], while Vision Transformers model long-range relationships through token interactions [@dosovitskiy2021vit]. The transformer design follows rotation-aware ideas where applicable from Krishnan et al. [@krishnan2024rvit].

The project adds two experimental modules:

- <Glossary term="PFD" definition="Pathology-Focused Disentanglement: a learned one-channel spatial gate inferred from the CNN feature map." /> estimates a soft spatial mask.
- <Glossary term="GSTE" definition="Guided Semantic Token Evolution: mean-normalised spatial guidance applied to transformer-facing feature or patch tokens." /> transfers that spatial preference into the token pathway.

The terminology is project-specific. The design is motivated by pathology-emphasis, hybrid CNN–Transformer, and token-selection literature [@xia2020pathology; @rao2021dynamicvit]; it should not be read as a claim that an established clinical concept has been reproduced.

### Stage 1: soft spatial gating

Let $F \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$ denote the CNN feature map and let $g(\cdot)$ be a learned mask head. PFD computes

$$
m = \sigma(g(F)), \qquad \widetilde{F} = F \odot m,
$$

where $m \in [0,1]^{1 \times H \times W}$ is a differentiable spatial gate and $\odot$ denotes broadcast element-wise multiplication.

Plain-language interpretation: the network learns a continuous spatial preference map rather than receiving a manual tumour segmentation mask.

### Stage 2: mean-normalised token guidance

The mask is resized to the token grid and normalised to preserve its mean scale:

$$
\alpha =
\frac{\operatorname{Resize}(m)}
{\operatorname{mean}(\operatorname{Resize}(m)) + \varepsilon},
\qquad
\widetilde{T} = T \odot \alpha.
$$

Variant A applies this mechanism to a fixed $7\times7$ CNN feature grid, giving 49 tokens. Variant B begins with $14\times14$ raw-image patches, giving 196 tokens, and can use concentration-aware weighted pooling toward a minimum $7\times7$ spatial side.

### Stage 3: late fusion

The CNN and transformer summaries remain separate until the classifier:

$$
z = [z_{\mathrm{cnn}};z_{\mathrm{vit}}],
\qquad
h = \operatorname{ReLU}(W_f z + b_f),
\qquad
\widehat{y} = W_o\!\left(\operatorname{Dropout}(h)\right)+b_o.
$$

This equation is the late-fusion formulation reported in the FYP.

| Property | Hybrid A | Hybrid B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial transformer tokens | 49 CNN feature tokens | 196 raw-image patch tokens |
| CNN descriptor | Ungated | PFD-gated |
| Guidance reach | Transformer-facing pathway | CNN and transformer pathways |
| Token count | Fixed | Optional concentration-aware pooling |
| Trainable parameters | 26.68M | 26.58M |

<Note title="Interpretation discipline" emoji="🔬" variant="neutral">
Because A and B differ in token source and guidance reach, cross-family comparisons are not clean estimates of one isolated design change. The strongest causal comparisons are Hybrid A versus Ablation A and Hybrid B versus Ablation B.
